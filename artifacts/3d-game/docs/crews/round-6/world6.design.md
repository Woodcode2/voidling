## The governor's recommendation

BUILD SKYLARK FIELD.

Your test is two questions, and the zoo and the balloon meet split them. Would a stranger stop on the frame? Both would — a ring of coloured kerbed cells round a black hole with stars through it is a beautiful still, and so is a forty-metre cobalt whale spilled across a runway at dawn with the shadows raking the opposite way to every other card you own. Call that a draw.

Could you cut an ad from it? That is where it stops being close. An ad needs something to HAPPEN in six seconds. The zoo's motion is a crowd circulating and animals walking politely away — you would have to invent the event and shoot it with a camera move. Skylark's event is the level itself: the balloons are authored in four stages held at roughly 5:4:3:2 and the ratio walks forward across the match, so the world stands up while the child eats it. Point a camera at a match and the ad cuts itself — flat crescents of colour lying on grey dewed grass, a fan, a burn, the whale rising, the void taking her in the two seconds after her basket leaves the ground. And the rule reads without sound, without text, in one shot: get them before they go up. No other concept here has a rule a stranger learns from the picture.

Second reason, and it is about where the money goes. The zoo asks for the first change to the island's topology in five worlds. A hole means a second coastline, and I checked the code: the cliff skirt walks the outline exactly once, so the inner rim would be an open nine-unit seam; the additive violet halo plane is a full rectangle centred under the island, so it would sit inside the hole and turn the hero image into a glowing lens instead of stars; and coastClear extrudes radially from the 3D origin, which on a ring points inland, so every prop on the inner lip certifies as clear of a coast it is hanging over. All three are fixable and all three are in shared geometry that five shipping worlds run through. Skylark's problems are arithmetic in a document — three runway thresholds drawn off the island, two designators that disagree with their own bearings, and half the island paved — and every one of them is fixed by redrawing before a line is written.

Third, the voice. Powder Pass is the world the child has just come from, and it is a clerk reading a list. The registrar reading a census is the same instrument with a different form. Pym is not: his denial is welded to the child's own play, because the more of the field gets eaten the flatter and clearer it becomes, so his instruments keep reporting that conditions are IMPROVING, and by his own criteria his last line is true. That is the game making a joke with the player rather than about them, and it is the only one of the six that does it.

What I am giving up by not picking the zoo: a giraffe is a more wanted thing than an airfield, and a stranger knows what a zoo is faster. My answer is that a hot air balloon is exactly as familiar as a giraffe and far rarer to stand next to, and the whale is both at once — and that "I ate the WHALE just as it was leaving" is a better thing for a child to say at school than "I ate the zoo".

Two conditions on the build, and neither is negotiable. Screenshot a standing envelope against the child's own void at 430x932 BEFORE the land module is written; if it occludes, the envelopes get shorter and the crown rule survives a shorter balloon. And fix the ascension accounting first: departed balloons must decrement the denominator, not count as devoured, or the end card credits the child with the sky.

## Rejected, and the one reason

- TULIP FLATS — the world's own one-line promise is not renderable. Nothing in this engine scars the ground: the stripes live in a baked texture, the void eats props, and "eat the flowers" leaves the flowers exactly where they were. It also opens, on purpose, on a grey coach park in a game judged on whether a stranger stops on that frame. It has the best picker card and the most rigorous silhouette argument in the set, and it still loses, because a card is not a match.
- RINGWELL — the ring is a one-world slot and three concepts wanted it; this one loses it to the zoo on subject, and its own numbers do not close. Outer oval 9,200 x 8,200 with a 3,000 hole leaves 5,706 units of land to split between the two sides, and the districts are written against 7,350 — it is 29% short of the town it describes, and its narrowest band lands under the corridor floor its own risk section sets. Its identity, warm light coming up from below, lives in RIG.hemiI, which I checked: pinned at 0.22 with the per-world column dead code that has never reached a pixel in any world.
- THE BIG LAP (RINGWOOD) — the same ring again, and the third English village fete in a set of six. Its institution is a lap board filing the apocalypse as achievement, which is warmer and funnier than Powder's closures desk but is the same instrument in a different hat, landing immediately after Powder in the ladder. Its engine research is the best in the set and is fully harvested below.

## To graft from the losers

- THE HALO PLANE (from THE BIG LAP, verified at island.ts:1035-1041). It is a full-bbox additive violet plane at y=-3 centred on the island. Any world with a hole in it must punch the hole through this plane too, or the hero image is a glowing lens where the stars were meant to be. This is the single graft that saves the zoo's whole pitch, and no other ring concept found it.
- THE CLIFF SKIRT WALKS THE OUTLINE ONCE (from THE BIG LAP, verified at island.ts:3388-3406). The underside cap is topGeo.clone() and inherits a hole; the 9-unit wall does not. A second coastline needs a second loop or the inner rim is an open seam on the one edge the world is about.
- coastClear IS RADIAL FROM THE 3D ORIGIN (found independently by THE BIG LAP, RINGWELL and FEEDING TIME, verified at island.ts:447). It tests a point pushed away from (0,0), so on any ring it pushes inland and certifies props standing on the inner lip. Every lamp, bar and scatter clearance call inherits it. Replace it with a distance-to-nearest-rim test and write the probe before the world.
- THE CORRECTED VOID ARITHMETIC (from THE BIG LAP, verified: FORM_MIN[5] = 8.0, R_CAP = 18). A WORLD ENDER is 320 world units across, not 640; a maxed void is 720. Use these numbers for every corridor and causeway claim in world 6 — three of the six concepts got them wrong in both directions.
- THE HERO PROP AS A CHAIN OF EDIBLES (from THE BIG LAP's viaduct). One merged mesh gets one eat radius and R_CAP caps it at ~20, which is why Game Day shipped a stadium nobody could swallow. This fixes Skylark's whale directly: 70 3D units nose to flukes, authored as 8-12 separately edible segments so the child eats her along her length.
- OPENWORK OVER EMPTY SPACE (from THE BIG LAP). A solid deck across a void photographs as a grey bar; girders with black showing between them read as a bridge. Applies to any span, and to the zoo's aviary tunnel.
- THE JEALOUS INSTITUTION (from RINGWELL). The place already HAS a hole; it is listed, it has a plaque and a committee, and the new one HAS NOT APPLIED. Possessiveness as denial is the best new comic engine in the six and it gets funnier on repeat, which is what a ticker needs. It grafts straight onto the zoo (the Drop, fenced since 1931, with a sign) and onto Skylark (an entrant with no registration letters and no ballot slot).
- TIER 2 MUST COMPLETE A CEREMONY, NOT READ A SHORTER LIST (from RINGWELL). Draw the raffle on air, with a number, a winner and nowhere left to claim from. That is the move structurally unavailable to a closures desk, and it is the fix for every clerical institution in this set.
- NOTHING SITS FLUSH (from RINGWELL's BRIM RULE). Legs touch y=0 so the placement probe's float category stays clean, and the body oversails them so light gets underneath and every prop draws its own line against the ground. General kit discipline, cheap, and it is the correct inverse of alpine's snow cap rather than a restatement of it.
- THE INSTRUMENT THAT HAS NEVER FINISHED (from RINGWELL's Hoggett, 418 years into a measurement). Skylark already has the shape of it and has not spent it: two windsocks that disagree, and a met balloon released once a match. Give one of them a number that has been going up since 1931.
- AUTHOR THE DENSITY, DO NOT SCATTER IT (from TULIP FLATS). A BED_ROWS-shaped generator in the mould of gameday's LOT_ROWS, then qa/_edcount.mjs BEFORE par is chosen. Skylark is the smallest island anyone proposed and needs this more than the world that invented it — Powder starved its child driver at 403 points against 3,253 on exactly this mistake.
- THE EAST KEY, MEASURED NOT ASSERTED (from TULIP FLATS). Every off vector in WORLD_LIGHT is negative-x — I checked all five — so a positive-x key flips every shadow in the game and is a real whole-frame difference. Tulip also demanded a framebuffer luminance sample before claiming the slot, which is the discipline Game Day's rig had to be rescued by. Skylark uses the same slot; take the measurement plan with it. And note for all six: RIG.hemiI is pinned at 0.22 and the per-world column is dead code, so no world may build its identity on the hemisphere until that is deliberately unlocked.
- THE CROWD FACES AWAY (from TULIP FLATS' two hundred people photographing the beds with their backs to the void). One bearing function, one gag, and it is the world's whole joke. Skylark's ticket-holders looking UP is the same move — make it the whole crowd, not one errand.
- DERIVE THE LONG AXIS FROM THE CAMERA (from TIDE TABLE). camOffset.x equals camOffset.z, so world south-west to north-east lies along the screen horizontal; a hero line laid on the other diagonal recedes down the barrel and disappears. Check Skylark's main runway against this before the polygon is drawn.
- THE CLOTH RULE (from TIDE TABLE). A bright surface hanging proud of a dark board reads from directly overhead as a continuous ribbon with a fine dark line down each edge. It is the direct heir to alpine's snow cap, it is enforceable per prop, and it is a better general rule than the painted-reflection system it was attached to.
- THE MODEL OF THE WORLD ON THE WORLD (from TIDE TABLE's cake). A sugar model of the whole island on top of the biggest meal, including a sugar version of itself, so the void eats the model of the world before it eats the world. Transplants to any landmark — the zoo's gift shop, Skylark's flea market table.
- THE TAIL (from FEEDING TIME). Animals are never eaten and never despawn: they FOLLOW, single file, behind the void. It SHOWS the game's standing promise instead of asserting it, it is the best overhead image proposed in the six, and every freed animal becomes an honest traveller for the crowd probe. Skylark's sheep do this down the runway.
- THE COUNT COMES OUT ONE OVER (from FEEDING TIME's sign-off). "Every animal present and correct. Every visitor present and correct. The count comes out one over. Everybody is accounted for, including the extra one." The game's standing promise delivered as the punchline — the best closing line in the six documents, and it belongs to whichever world ships.
- THE ROPED CHAIN (from FEEDING TIME). One adult with a real destination and seven children inheriting its heading: seven free travellers for the crowd probe and a lovely overhead image. This is exactly the formation code Skylark's four-crew basket carry needs and does not have.
- A PEN IS READ BY ITS FLOOR, NOT ITS FENCE (from FEEDING TIME). From directly overhead a rail is a hairline; a hard-edged substrate patch inside a thick painted kerb reads as a contained place from three hundred units up. Skylark's painted launch circle and threshold markings are the same rule — take the enforcement clause with it: a pen with a fence and no kerb is not finished.

# WORLD 6 — THE SHORTLIST

Six designers, six worlds, one slot. Here is what to build, what to take from the ones we are not building, and the evidence, so you can overrule me from the same page I decided on.

---

## THE THING THAT DECIDED HALF OF THIS

Three of the six proposed the same island: a ring with a hole punched clean through it and stars showing underneath. RINGWELL, THE BIG LAP and FEEDING TIME are the same silhouette with a village fete, a fun run and a zoo laid on top of it.

That is a one-world slot. We are not building two holes. So the panel's first job was to decide which subject deserves the hole, and the answer is the zoo — a six-year-old wants a zoo more than an English village fete, and wants it twice as much as a second English village fete. The other two are on the reject list and both have been stripped for parts; between them they found three real bugs in our shared geometry that whoever builds a hole world would otherwise have discovered on the first render.

## THE SHORTLIST

### 1. SKYLARK FIELD — *"get them before they go up"*
*(proposed as DAWN PATROL; rename it, the shelf is place names and "Dawn Patrol" is a war-film title)*

A disused grass-and-concrete airfield floating in space. Once a year a hundred hot air balloons come to it, and a match is that morning compressed to three minutes: trailers arriving in the dark, envelopes spilled across the grass, the mass ascension at sunrise. The hero is THE WHALE — a forty-metre cobalt special-shape balloon lying across the launch circle for the first half of the match, standing up in the second, and leaving at the end.

**The rule.** Eat the balloon first and you get the balloon. Eat the ropes first and it goes up without you, and everybody waves at it. A child works that out in ten seconds and explains it to another child at school.

**Silhouette: holds.** A three-armed island bitten INWARD in three places — every other island we ship bulges outward everywhere — with three straight pale concrete bars crossing the middle and a perimeter track drawing the coastline on the island, so the outline survives even where the coast falls into dawn shadow. Nobody confuses it with the blob, the hook, the plateau, the valley or the bowl.

**And the shape MOVES.** The balloons are authored in four stages — bagged, spilled, cold, standing — held at about 5:4:3:2, and the ratio walks forward across the match. No other world's composition changes while you play it. That is where the ad is.

**Institution.** Mr Pym, the Balloonmeister, deciding whether it is safe to fly. His instruments are excellent and they are answering the wrong question. As the child eats, the field gets flatter, emptier and clearer — so his instruments keep reporting that conditions are IMPROVING, and his last line ("the finest flying conditions in the history of the meet") is, by his own criteria, true.

**Cost.** The ordinary sixth-world bill and no rewrite of shared code: a land module around 350 lines (bay is 351, powder 341, gameday 419), a prop kit between alpine's 766 and nightmarket's 1,125, a newsroom of 700–1,300 (powder's is 709, maple's 1,299), the island build-and-bake block, a crowd block, and the tail — light rig, hours, par, season, stickers, music, picker card, qa world lists. Specific extras: the whale as a segmented hero, formation movement for the four-crew basket carry, a look-up pose, and a third prop state in the score accounting.

**What must change before a line is written.**
- **Three of six runway thresholds are drawn off the island**, and two of three runway designators disagree with their own bearings. Redraw and renumber.
- **Half the island is pavement.** It is the smallest island proposed (~35 Mu2 against Powder's 45) and three runways at half-width 500 cover 50% of it. Keep ONE hero runway at full width — being wider than a maxed-out void is the right call and worth keeping — and make the other two disused broken slab with grass through it. That recovers about ten million square units of dressable ground, and cracked concrete is better dressing than clean concrete.
- **Screenshot the occlusion at 430×932 first.** A twenty-metre standing envelope at our camera angle can hide the child's own void. If it does, the envelopes get shorter; the crown rule survives a shorter balloon.
- **Fix the ascension accounting.** A departed balloon must decrement the denominator, not count as devoured, or the end card credits the child with the sky. And the departures need a long telegraph, one at a time, never a wave, with a hard rule that the last handful never leave.
- **Six beats, not four.** The match deals two middles from a per-world pool. And replace beat 1 ("Filling the balloons") — it is the only opener in the game that does not change the screen. Make it the first balloon of the morning actually going up, which teaches the level's whole rule at thirty seconds.

**Scores: child 9 · engine 6 · author 8.** The highest child score and the highest author score in the set.

### 2. FEEDING TIME (Crater Park Zoo) — *"everything gets fed at nine · eat the whole zoo"*

A zoo built in a complete ring around THE DROP: a circular hole clean through the island, fenced since 1931, with a viewing platform, a coin telescope pointed at nothing and a sign reading PLEASE DO NOT FEED THE DROP. The void does not arrive at this world. It climbs out of the middle of it.

**Silhouette: holds, because of the hole.** In frame one there is open space with stars in it in the centre of the screen, and no repaint of anything we ship can produce that. But the hole is currently doing all the work: the outer boundary swings only 4,250–4,750, which is a circle at thumbnail size, and an oval composed round an off-centre ellipse is — in plan view — Powder Pass. Push the south-west band past 5,200 and pull the north-east in NOW, while it is free, so the crescent survives even if the hole is ever shrunk in a containment scare.

**Institution.** The Duty Registrar reading the morning stock-take. The only institution in the game that never gets a fact wrong, and it believes a correctly recorded thing is a handled thing, so the end of the world arrives in the notes column. Its closing line — "The count comes out one over. Everybody is accounted for, including the extra one" — is the best sign-off anyone wrote. Its problem is that it is the nearest neighbour to Powder's closures desk, and Powder is the world the child just came from.

**Cost.** The ordinary bill plus the most expensive engineering on the shortlist, all of it in shared code: a hole in the ground shape, the cliff skirt run a second time over the inner rim, the halo plane punched through, coastClear rewritten, and a kerbed enclosure floor as real geometry on a flat plane — which nothing in this game does today and which is the level's most-repeated new object.

**What must change.** Decide on paper, before code, what happens when a child drives the void at a giraffe — this is the one setting where the most-wanted object is the forbidden one and it will be tested in the first thirty seconds. The answer proposed is the right one and should be adopted: animals are never eaten, they FOLLOW, single file, behind the void. Also: make the finale the feed. The world is called FEEDING TIME and none of its four beats is the feed.

**Scores: child 8 · engine 6 · author 7.**

### 3. TIDE TABLE — *"one table. six islands. eat up."*

A cold northern estuary on the one morning a year the sea goes all the way out. Six island villages have built a claim to the world record for the longest continuous table — one unbroken table, laid for breakfast, running island to island across the uncovered flats, and it must be finished and fully occupied before the tide comes back.

**Silhouette: does NOT hold as written — and here is the change that fixes it.** Because the flats are walkable ground, the outline against space is one ragged waisted basin, which is the same family as Lantern's valley and Powder's bowl. The six-island read is entirely internal, painted into the ground bake, which means a lighting failure erases the whole idea. **Fix: cut the flats out of the walkable set except for four broad silt causeways.** Then the island is genuinely six masses on four necks, the plural read is in the shape rather than the paint, and the mirrored silt beyond becomes coastline exactly the way the sea does in Pirate Bay. It costs dressable ground and a fresh edible count, and it is the only way this concept earns a top-three place.

**What is genuinely first-rate here.** The white line threading all six islands is the best wayfinding in the game — no arrow, no UI, and "follow the white line to the cake" is a rule a child already knows from a birthday party. The cake carries a sugar model of all six islands with the table running across them, including a sugar cake, so the void eats the model of the world before the world. And the Adjudicator is the first OUTSIDER institution we have had: everyone else is the place's own machinery lying about it; he arbitrates, and his denial is impartiality — the one man forbidden from panicking because panicking would be taking the town's side.

**Risks.** It is the second world with pale ground, boats and a shoreline in the thumbnail; take the picker card to art direction against Pirate Bay before any code, and if they rhyme, move it inland to a drawn-down reservoir and lose the sea entirely — the record does not need a tide, only a deadline. It also wants the flattest, lowest-key rig anyone proposed, in a repo that has twice had to rescue a world for exactly that. And it has no cast: four thousand people and not one of them named.

**Scores: child 7 · engine 6 · author 7.**

## WHAT WE TAKE FROM THE LOSERS

**Three real bugs, found by the rejected ring concepts, that any hole world would otherwise have shipped:** the halo plane is a full rectangle and would fill the hole with violet glow instead of stars; the cliff skirt walks the outline once and would leave the inner rim an open seam; coastClear extrudes radially from the origin and would certify props hanging over the hole. Also the corrected void arithmetic — a WORLD ENDER is 320 world units across, not 640 — which three of the six concepts got wrong in both directions.

**Two art rules worth keeping whatever we build:** nothing sits flush (legs on the ground, body oversailing, so light gets underneath and every prop draws its own line), and a pen is read by its floor, not its fence (a hard substrate patch inside a thick painted kerb) — which is exactly what Skylark's painted launch circle and threshold markings already want to be.

**Four writing grafts:** the jealous institution ("it has not applied"); tier 2 must COMPLETE a ceremony rather than read a shorter list; "the count comes out one over — everybody is accounted for, including the extra one"; and the instrument that has never finished its measurement, which Skylark can spend on two windsocks that disagree.

**Three crowd grafts:** the tail of animals following the void in single file (it SHOWS the promise instead of asserting it); the roped chain of eight behind one adult, which is precisely the formation code Skylark's four-crew basket carry needs and does not have; and the whole crowd facing away by construction.

**And three method grafts:** author the density with a row generator and count the edibles BEFORE par is chosen; derive the world's long axis from the camera so the hero line lies across the frame instead of down the barrel; and measure the new light rig against a framebuffer sample before claiming the slot is free — with the standing note that the hemisphere term is pinned and no world may build its identity on it until we deliberately unlock it.

## APPENDIX — THE SIX CONCEPTS IN FULL

### TULIP FLATS — from the "silhouette" angle

**everybody came to see the flowers · eat the flowers**

"A flower farm on a drained polder, on the first morning of the season, twenty minutes after sunrise. The fields opened at six, nine coaches have already arrived, and four hundred people are standing in the lanes photographing thirty beds of red, yellow, pink and white while a small purple hole works its way up the staircase behind them. The whole place is run by ONE RULE — keep to the path — and the man with the loud-hailer will spend three minutes politely, patiently, and without ever once raising his voice, asking the world ender to observe it."

- **Silhouette.** "A STAIRCASE. Three straight-edged, ruled rectangular compartments stepping away to the north-west, each offset about 700 units north-east of the one before, joined by 1,900-unit open gaps, with a single narrow causeway running off the top step into black space and a windmill on its tip. Every edge is a hand-drawn line, because reclaimed land IS drawn with a ruler. TREAD ONE, THE NEW POLDER: centre (8500, 8750), 3,400 x 3,100, rows ruled at 22 degrees. TREAD TWO: centre (6450, 6500), 3,200 x 2,900, rows at 34 degrees. ### RINGWELL — from the "palette" angle

**mind the hole**

A small chalk-and-terracotta town built in a complete circle around a hole that goes all the way through the island, and out the other side into space. Nobody knows how deep it is; a man called Hoggett has been measuring it since 1608. Today is RAILINGS DAY, the one afternoon a year the great amber planet directly beneath passes right under the hole, so light comes UP through the middle of town — and the whole town brings out trestle tables, bunting, a tombola and a tea urn to sit around the rim and enjoy it. The joke is that the town already has a hole. It is listed. It has a plaque and a committee and a set of railings. When a second, smaller, purple hole turns up and starts eating the marquees, the Preservation Society's objection is not that it is dangerous — it is that it has not applied.

- **Silhouette.** A RING. An annulus: an irregular oval of land roughly 9,200 x 8,200 world units (outer edge sweeping from ~[1400,1950] to ~[10600,10150], centred near [6000,6050]) with a genuine HOLE punched clean through it — THE WELL, a near-circle about 3,000 across centred at [5350,5250], pushed north-west of the island's centre so the ring is fat in the south-east (band ~4,900, the fete field) and pinched in the north-west (band ~2,450, one terrace of houses). No water anywhere; the hole's edge is out-of-bounds exactly the way the sea is in bay.ts, so insideIslandWorld becomes pointInPoly(outer) && !pointInPoly(inner) and the flood-fill stays one connected loop at 100%. What it refuses, explicitly: Maple's fat blob on a grid (this has a hole in it), Pirate Bay's hook throwing an arm (this is closed and returns to itself), Game Day's lopsided plateau (this is not a mass, it is a band), Lantern's waisted corridor-valley with one sightline north (this world has no straight sightline at all — the street CURVES and the horizon bends), and Powder's oval bowl (that one is solid and organic and composed round a lake you can walk on; this one is geometric, man-made, and composed round a void you cannot). Powder is the nearest neighbour and the difference is not a matter of degree: there is nothing in the middle of this island.
- **Why it is a new shape.** Because in the first second you see stars through it. Every other world is a shape with a middle; this one is a shape with a missing middle, and the missing middle is the sky. The camera is pitched 46 degrees at spawn on a 32-degree lens looking down the 225 bearing, and the hole sits near frame centre — so the establishing shot is a town-sized ring of pale flagstone with a black-and-amber disc of open space punched through it and an iron arm cantilevered out across the gap. No repaint of an existing land polygon can produce that; you either have a hole or you do not. The secondary tell arrives half a second later: the town's plan is a WHEEL — a ring road round the outside, a promenade round the inside, and eleven radial lanes joining them — which reads at thumbnail size as concentric circles with spokes, where Maple reads as a grid, Pirate as a hook, Game Day as a blot and Lantern as a stripe.
- **Landmark.** THE WINDING HOUSE AND THE ARM. A tall lime-washed brick engine house on the northern pinch — verdigris copper roof with a deep brim, a fifteen-metre black iron wheel turning slowly on its gable, and a lattice ARM cantilevered 900 world units out over the well carrying a bucket on a chain that has been going down since 1608 and has never once come back up. It is the biggest meal in the level and the last thing eaten, and its precinct polygon swallows the yard, the chain store and the foot of the arm, so eating the building takes the deck out from under it rather than leaving a rind (the lesson gameday.ts, lantern.ts and powder.ts all record). Two numbers, not one: WIND = {cx:5600, cy:2700, rx:1150, ry:820} is the precinct, WIND_MESH_K = 0.42 is the building. From directly overhead it is the only object in the game silhouetted against open space rather than against ground — a pale square, a bright circle and a black line crossing the hole. When it goes, the arm falls into the well, and the newspaper the next morning records that the bucket is now officially overdue.
- **Palette and light.** CHALK 0xf2e6cf (warm bone flagstone, the ground state), STRAW 0xd8b45c (dry midsummer meadow), TERRACOTTA 0xc4563a (every pantile roof), VERDIGRIS 0x4e9c84 (copper roofs, railings, the wheel) — plus IRON 0x3a3f52 as the only dark, and one glow, G_WELL 0xffa54e, which is the colour of the light coming up the hole and of the planet in the sky, deliberately the same value. TIME OF DAY: the top of a high-summer day — the highest sun in the game and the brightest world in the game. The slot is free and here is the proof rather than the claim. Maple's noon is a WARM key (0xfff2d8, 1.75) at 54 degrees over a COOL bounce (hemiGround 0x4a4468) with an untinted violet nebula and exposure 1.0. Pirate is the same rig with a bluer fill. Game Day is 0xffd9a8 at 40 degrees, dusk 0.45. Lantern has no sun. Powder is a cold 0xd8e6ff key at 50 degrees, dusk 0.85. Nothing in WORLD_LIGHT has a key above ~60 degrees, nothing has a near-neutral key, and — the real gap — every daylight world's hemiGround is a dark or cold bounce, because on Earth the ground is darker than the sky. This island is over a lit planet, so its bounce is a saturated ORANGE and the world is warmer underneath than above. That inversion is what lantern.ts identifies as the trick that stops night reading as "the day but darker"; nobody has run it in full daylight, and it is the whole look.
- **The overhead rule.** THE BRIM RULE. Alpine's rule is that a building is mostly its roof, so cap the roof in snow and leave one dark slate strip at the eave — the dark strip is what says the white is SITTING ON something. This world needs the inverse, and it needs it for two stacked reasons that are both the camera's fault. (1) Looking straight down you see a roof and the floor; you never see a wall. (2) The sun here is at 67 degrees, so shadows are about 0.42 of an object's height and tuck almost entirely underneath — which means the shadow that separated a building from the floor in every other world is GONE. So: EVERY ROOF MUST OVERSAIL ITS WALLS BY A REAL MARGIN — a deep eave, a brim, an awning lip, a table skirt, a canopy valance — because the fill light comes from BELOW and paints a hot amber line on the underside of that oversail. From the play camera a building here is a pale terracotta tile with a glowing edge, and the edge is the whole trick: a flat-sided box has no brim, catches no underlight, gets no shadow from a near-vertical sun, and dissolves into the flagstones. THE COROLLARY, which is checkable and which every factory in the kit must obey: NOTHING SITS FLUSH. Trestles, benches, sheds, the bandstand, the shop fronts and the marquee poles all stand on legs, blocks, staddle stones or a plinth — the legs touch y=0 so qa/placement.mjs's `float` category stays at zero, and the body floats above them so the well's light gets underneath and draws each prop its own warm line. If a factory in this file has a roof and no brim, or a base and no legs, the factory is not finished.
- **Institution in denial.** THE RINGWELL AND DISTRICT PRESERVATION SOCIETY — the fete's own public address: a folding table, one microphone, a horn speaker on a pole, and the PROGRAMME OF EVENTS, read out in order by a volunteer named Mrs. Pell who has read it out every year for thirty-one years. Not a mayor (Maple), not resort management (Pirate), not two commentators (Game Day), not a recording that cannot perceive damage (Lantern), not a closures desk reading a list (Powder). This one's denial is PROCEDURAL-ENTHUSIAST: it cannot report an emergency, because an emergency is not an item — so everything that happens is entered as an item and given a time. Tier 0, DOUBT: the programme proceeds; the second hole is announced as "the unscheduled attraction" and the Society wishes to stress it is not affiliated and has not been risk-assessed. Zero exclamation marks in tier 0, by rule — the format never breaks. Tier 1, ALARM: the Society believes it now, so it does the only thing it knows how to do, which is AMEND THE PROGRAMME. Items are brought forward. "3:15, Tombola, brought forward. 3:16, Tombola." Tier 2, PANIC: broadcasting from the last trestle table on the last yard of Railing Walk, still reading the programme, which now has one item left on it, and it is the raffle, and the raffle WILL be drawn. Running grievance, in every tier: the town already has a hole, it is listed, it has a plaque, and this new one has not applied. Cast: Mrs. Pell; Hoggett the Well Keeper, four hundred and eighteen years into a measurement; Denise, tombola; the Vicar, who has the tickets; and a child called Ivy who has been told twice not to lean on the railings. Nobody is hurt, everybody is accounted for, and the paper confirms it.
- **Districts.** THE LONG GREEN (south-east, widest band, density 1.4) — the fete field: striped marquees, forty trestle tables, hay bales, the tombola and the coconut shy. The spawn is here, in the middle of the tea queue, facing the hole.; THE PARADE (east, 1.2) — the town's one street, a crescent of lime-washed shops under deep canvas awnings that follows the ring instead of running away from you; you never see the end of it, only the next forty degrees.; RAILING WALK (the entire inner rim, 1.1) — the promenade around the well: iron railings, heritage plaques, coin-operated telescopes angled DOWN, and every bench in the district facing inward at nothing. The one district shaped like the world.; THE WINDING HOUSE (north, on the pinch, 0.8) — the landmark's precinct: its cobbled yard, its chain store, the foot of the arm, and a rope barrier nobody has crossed in a hundred years.; THE TERRACE (north-west, narrowest band at ~2,450, 1.3) — a single row of tall three-storey pantiled houses with their backs to the outer cliff and their front doorsteps on the walk, so residents open their doors onto the void.; THE ALLOTMENTS (south-west, 1.0) — sheds, bean poles, water butts, wheelbarrows, and a marrow under a cloche on a cushion that is going to win.; THE PUMP YARD (west, 0.9) — the ironmongery that maintains the pretence that this is a well: a fifteen-metre verdigris wheel, pipework going nowhere, the pump house, a tarpaulin.; THE ORCHARD RIM (north-east and the outer band all round, 0.5) — dry orchard, beehives and a mown edge thinning to the cliff. Listed last, so it is the catch-all any un-districted ground falls to.
- **Beats.** 🫖 | The urn is on! | a queue has already formed · 🥒 | Best marrow! | it is not close · 🪣 | The bucket came back! | four hundred years. empty. · 🌅 | THE SHINE!! | the light is coming up the well
- **Errands.** The fete is a CIRCUIT, and that is what makes the numbers work rather than a retrofit. life.ts's `leg` is opt-in per call site (ERR.LEASH 3.0, D0/D1 dwell 2.5/6.0), and here almost every mover gets one, because on a ring an errand leg is an ARC or a CHORD and never a random walk — the geometry itself produces drift. Four crowds. (1) THE LAP, ~40% of the fete-goers, leg 6-8: they are walking the whole way round Railing Walk, anticlockwise, because that is what you do — a person covering a 200-degree arc of a 1,500-radius promenade nets enormous displacement against path length, and this group alone carries the median past 0.30. (2) THE STALL RUN, leg 3-5: gate, then the urn, then the produce tent, then the tombola, then the coconut shy, then back to the urn, with real dwell at each — every destination is a tagged prop (qk: 'urn' | 'tombola' | 'produce' | 'shy' | 'telescope' | 'bandstand' | 'busstop' | 'plotgate' | 'windingsteps'), so a journey (15 units travelled, then settled within 3 for 1.5s) completes at every stop. (3) THE STALLHOLDERS, leg 1-2: behind the trestle, to the urn, back behind the trestle — short legs, high dwell, they are the district's furniture but they are furniture that goes and gets a cup of tea. (4) THE ORBIT, leg 4-6, the ones with somewhere to be: Society volunteers in hi-vis walking the ring with clipboards, the Silver Band carrying instruments from the bandstand to the green and back, and Hoggett walking from the Winding House to the railings to look down, every ninety seconds, forever. Two crowd behaviours are world-specific: everyone on Railing Walk faces INWARD when idle (rwFacingWell(wx,wy) is the world's facing function, the analogue of gdFacingStadium and pwFacingLodge), and on the SHINE beat the entire cast turns to the rim at once — the only moment in the game when several hundred people all look the same way, at a hole, while a smaller hole eats the marquees behind them.
- **Prop kit.** bunting run — a strung line of triangles between two anchors, the ring's graphic signature from overhead, trestle table with a cloth skirt, on X legs (the skirt is what catches the underlight), tea urn on a stand, tin mugs, a hand-lettered price card, striped marquee, deep scalloped eaves, guy ropes, mud-free grass under it, tombola drum on a cranked stand, coconut shy — three cups, canvas back, a bucket of balls, the bandstand — octagonal, verdigris roof with a real brim, eight cast legs, empty music stands, railing run + heritage plaque on a slanted stone, coin-operated telescope, angled DOWN into the well, inward-facing bench, seat oversailing its legs, THE WINDING HOUSE (landmark), the arm + bucket + chain (landmark part, overhangs the void), lime-washed shop front with a canvas awning and a brim, terrace house — tall, narrow, three storeys, pantile, doorstep on the walk, allotment shed + water butt + bean-pole wigwam, the prize marrow, on a cushion, under a cloche, stacked hay bales, wheelbarrow of cut flowers, the pump wheel — iron, verdigris, half-sunk in its yard, the Society's folding table with a horn speaker on a pole, hand-painted signpost on a stake (WELL, 3 min, an arrow pointing down), striped deckchair, ice-cream trike with a fringed parasol, red pillar box — the one hard red that is not a roof, beehive row, the single-decker bus, parked, NOT IN SERVICE, nobody in it
- **Risk, in the designer's own words.** Six honest ones, worst first. (1) THE HOLE IS THE FIRST CHANGE TO THE ISLAND'S TOPOLOGY IN FIVE WORLDS. The ground is a THREE.Shape into ShapeGeometry (island.ts:3070) so the hole itself is one shape.holes.push of a reversed inner path and triangulates for free, and the underside cap is topGeo.clone() so it inherits the hole — but the cliff skirt at island.ts:3388 walks sil3 once to build a wall from y=0 to y=-9, and this world needs it to walk the inner ring too, wound the other way, or the well is a cut-out with no thickness. insideIslandWorld, coastClear (which extrudes radially from the ORIGIN — and the origin of this world is inside the hole, so coastClear's assumption inverts and must be reworked for the inner rim), the coast-dressing sample points, the surf/rim bake band and every containment probe all need re-running rather than assuming. This is bounded work, not open-ended, but it is real and it is the largest engine cost in the design. (2) THE PLANET-IN-THE-HOLE IS A CLAIM, NOT A MEASUREMENT. The SKIES band is visible from about -50 to -81 degrees elevation and the body is placed at el -62 on the rig azimuth so it lands behind and below the island's centre — but whether it actually frames inside the hole depends on camera pitch (46 at spawn, 65 at VOID TITAN), on the well's offset from the 225 bearing, and on the equirect dome's parallax as the camera pulls from ~50 to 340 units. qa/skypop.mjs and qa/skygrid.mjs are the instruments and they must run before anyone repeats this sentence. If it cannot be framed, the world still works and loses its single best image. (3) THE MASCOT CEILING IS NOT ONLY EXPOSURE. formsep measured him at rgb(119-122, 62-66, 199-200) across five worlds, max pairwise CIE76 2.49 against MIN_DE 6. Exposure 1.22 sits inside the derived 1.26 ceiling with margin — but this rig also moves the key's elevation to 67 degrees and puts a saturated orange directional under him, and neither is covered by that derivation. His rendered colour WILL move. fillI 0.66 is the number to give back if he drifts, not the exposure. (4) A 67-DEGREE SUN IS THE SHORTEST SHADOW IN THE GAME (~0.42x height) and therefore the least grounding the key has ever provided. The contact-shadow discs carry it — placement.mjs skips them as a class, so they exist independently — but qa/grounding.mjs is the gate and it should be run before the art is defended. (5) BRIGHT + WARM + A STRONG WARM BOUNCE IS ONE STEP FROM ORANGE MUSH, the same way alpine is one step from whiteout. Verdigris and iron are not decoration in this palette, they are the two things standing between a gold world and a beige one, and if a colour census comes back with under about 12% cool coverage the world is failing. (6) A RING MAKES EVERY JOURNEY A LAP. If traversal reads as a chore in playtest, the corrective is the well's OFFSET (push it further north-west, fatten the south-east) and never the band's width — a band under about 2,400 world units puts a WORLD ENDER, which is ~700 across, into a corridor, and this world must never have one.
- **Poster.** Painted key art, 3:4, chunky hand-modelled clay-and-gouache register matching the five existing posters — soft rounded forms, visible brush texture, no line art, no photoreal detail. High three-quarter aerial view, camera looking down at about 50 degrees, of a small circular town floating alone in space: a complete RING of warm chalk-white and straw-gold land, terracotta pantile roofs and verdigris copper domes crowding both its edges, with a clean HOLE punched straight through the middle of the island and open starry space visible through it. Deep in that hole, seen through it and slightly beyond, an enormous banded amber gas giant fills the gap and throws warm orange light UP out of the well, so the inner rim of the town, the undersides of every eave and awning, and the faces of the buildings turned toward the centre all glow hot amber, while their roofs stay pale and cool under a high near-white summer sun. Iron railings run the whole inner rim with tiny figures leaning on them. Bunting in red, white and blue triangles is strung from the railings out to the shopfronts in radiating spokes; striped marquees and trestle tables crowd the wide southern arc; an octagonal green-roofed bandstand sits among them. At the top of the ring, on its narrowest point, stands the hero: a tall lime-washed brick winding house with a black iron wheel on its gable and a lattice arm cantilevered out over the hole, a bucket hanging from it on a chain, sharply silhouetted against the planet below. Just off centre on the flagstones near the rim, small but unmistakable, the little purple void with its happy face, one marquee already half gone beside it. Sky: dark cobalt space, dense faint starfield, no atmosphere haze — the ground is far brighter than the sky. Palette strictly chalk white, straw gold, terracotta red, verdigris green, hot amber glow, deep cobalt. Cheerful, sunlit, warm, entirely non-threatening; nobody in danger, everyone having a lovely afternoon.

### TIDE TABLE — from the "voice" angle

**one table. six islands. eat up.**

A cold northern estuary on the one morning a year the sea goes all the way out. Six island villages have spent eleven months building a claim to the world record for the LONGEST CONTINUOUS TABLE — one unbroken table, laid for breakfast, running island to island across the uncovered flats, and it must be finished, laid and fully occupied before the tide comes back. Four thousand people are carrying plates to numbered seats at dawn. Standing beside the table with a clipboard and a stopwatch is an adjudicator from the record-keeping body, who is present to verify the claim, who is not from here, and who will be going home tonight either way. He is asked repeatedly about the hole. He declines to comment on the hole, because the hole has not entered, and he is not here to have opinions. He is here to rule on whether the record stands.

- **Silhouette.** An ARCHIPELAGO: six islands of dark sea-green strung on a bright anti-diagonal across a wide, ragged tidal basin, joined by four raised causeways, with one white line — the table — threading through every one of them from a start post on the smallest island to the cake at the far end. What it refuses, by name: Maple Isle's fat blob on a road grid; Pirate Bay's hooked headland cradling water; GAME DAY's broad flat plateau with its lopsided lobe and apron; Lantern Night's waisted valley with a canal down it; and Powder Pass's concentric oval bowl. Every one of those five is a SINGLE closed organic outline drawn in one stroke. This one is six outlines and the gaps between them, and the gaps are the shape. THE ISLANDS ARE THE SILHOUETTE; THE FLATS ARE THE SEA THAT IS NOT THERE. The basin runs roughly 8,600 world units along its long axis by 5,400 across, flaring open to the north-east at the estuary mouth and pinching to a river channel at the south-west, with two headlands squeezing the middle so the outer edge never closes into an oval. THE CHAIN IS LAID SOUTH-WEST TO NORTH-EAST, and that is not a taste decision: camOffset is (0.62, 0.92, 0.62) normalised (prototype3d.ts:626), and because camOffset.x equals camOffset.z the ground basis is a flat 45 degrees off the world axes — prototype3d.ts:9255 derives screen-right as (camOffset.z, -camOffset.x), which puts world south-west-to-north-east exactly along the screen's horizontal. The record's whole length therefore lies ACROSS the frame instead of vanishing into it. Laid on the other diagonal it would recede to a point and the biggest idea in the level would be invisible from the only camera the game has.
- **Why it is a new shape.** Because in the first second the eye counts SIX THINGS instead of resolving ONE. Every other world in this game is a continuous field: you land, you read a single coastline, and everything inside it is equally near. Here the frame is broken into bright islands on a mirror, and the first read is plural. Nothing else in the game is plural. Then the second read is the line — one white thread running through all six, which is the only thing in the frame that crosses the gaps, and which tells the child where to go without a single arrow. And the third read is the rhythm, which is the actual design: dense, empty, dense, empty, six times. On an island the table SWITCHBACKS in tight boustrophedon rows to use every metre of ground (that is what a real record attempt does), so an island from overhead is a hatched block of white. Across the flats it runs dead straight, one line on an empty mirror. Maple, Pirate, GAME DAY, Lantern and Powder are all one continuous density gradient from a hero district out to a rim. THIS WORLD'S STRUCTURE IS ITS PACING: eat the hatched blocks for a fast, tight, high-scoring flurry; commit to the long straight crossing for a lean run with nothing either side of you. That alternation is authored into the plan, it is legible from directly overhead, and no repaint of an existing island could produce it. And it is achieved with ONE CONTINUOUS WALKABLE LANDMASS — the flats are ground, not water — so there is not one invisible wall anywhere in it. That is the direct descendant of powder.ts's frozen lake ("walkable ground painted as ice... onIce() tells the physics where to slide; it never tells the containment where to stop") and it is the same trick pushed to the scale of a whole world.
- **Landmark.** THE CAKE, on the head table at the north-east end. Five iced tiers, taller than the chapel, under a striped canopy on the widest table in the level — and on the top tier, in sugar, a model of all six islands with the table running across them, including a sugar head table with a sugar cake on it. The void eats the model of the world before it eats the world. It is the biggest single mesh in the level and it is the last thing eaten, and it is approached in a way nothing else in the game is approached: not down a corridor (Lantern's bathhouse), not up an open slope from below (Powder's lodge), not across an apron (GAME DAY's stadium), but ALONG the authored line you have been following since the first second. And the line is not a corridor — the flats are open on both sides the whole way. It pulls; it does not contain.
- **Palette and light.** DAWN — the sun on the horizon behind sea-mist, and this is the only rig in the game where THE SKY OUT-LIGHTS THE SUN. Four colours. (1) MIRROR ROSE-PEWTER, the wet flats, which are simply the sky doubled: rose-gold toward the light, cool pewter in the shadowed runnels, and it is the biggest surface in every frame. (2) CLOTH WHITE, the table, the one pure bright value in the level and the only thing that crosses the gaps. (3) SIGNAL RED, carried hard and everywhere — bunting, jam pots, the chapel door, the lobster pots, buoys, the adjudicator's ribbon — doing exactly the job alpine.ts assigns sled red and gritter orange, which is standing between a pastel world and a wash-out. (4) DEEP SEA-GREEN SLATE, the island grass, the boat hulls, the weed on the causeway posts: the dark value everything bright sits on. Plus warm window glow, because every kitchen in the estuary has been up since three. WHY THE SLOT IS FREE, checked against WORLD_LIGHT (prototype3d.ts:734) and HOURS (:907): maple and pirate are neutral daylight, sun 0xfff2d8 at 1.75 with hemi 0.5; gameday is the golden-hour rake, sun 0xffd9a8 at 2.55, off [-38,72,78] (40 degrees, deliberately raised from 26 because a low sun in a dense district shades the whole level), dusk 0.45; lantern has no sun at all, a 0.42 moon; powder is blue winter dusk. Every daylight rig in the game is KEY-DOMINANT with the hemisphere at 0.5-0.86. Dawn here inverts that: sun weak and low (sunI around 1.0, a pale rose), hemiSky a rose-gold and hemiGround a warm silt at hemiI around 1.3, the highest in the game; dusk around 0.7 so every window, lantern and oven mouth burns from the first frame; the cool counter-light (WorldLight.fill) swung hard and warm-opposed, because with a weak key the fill is doing most of the form separation. Exposure caps at 1.15, INSIDE the 1.24 ceiling ruled on 2026-08-28 — the mascot must stay one colour across all six worlds and his response is world-independent at about 0.22 dE per exposure point. Fog is a per-world knob already (island.ts SKY_MOOD, scene.fog set at :629): a rose-pewter mist rather than powder's slate 0x1a2742, and the mist is free depth on a world made of distances.
- **The overhead rule.** NOTHING STANDS ON THE FLATS WITHOUT ITS REFLECTION — AND THE REFLECTION NEVER TOUCHES THE THING. The camera looks down, so on this world the ground is not scenery, it is most of the picture, and it is a mirror. An object standing on a mirror with nothing under it does not read as an object; it reads as a sticker pasted onto a photograph, which is this world's exact equivalent of nightmarket's bathhouse photographing as a black rectangle. And the obvious fix is closed: island.ts:3275 records a measured negative result — dropping the ground roughness from 0.97 to 0.45 changes ZERO pixels by more than 3/255, and even at 0.05, a near mirror, only 5.3% of the frame moves at all, because the ground is one flat horizontal plane whose single mirror direction does not point at this camera. A WET GROUND IS NOT REACHABLE THROUGH ROUGHNESS HERE. So the reflection is PAINTED, into the ground bake, exactly the way Lantern Night paints its lantern pools ("THE LIGHT IS IN THE GROUND", island.ts:1502) at each lantern's own position so the source and its pool agree. The rule, mechanically: every prop sited on the flats gets a REFLECTION STROKE laid into the bake at its own foot, running away from the sun's azimuth; length equals the prop's height times 1.6 in world units; opacity 0.34, feathered to zero over the last third; colour is the prop's two loudest colours mixed 40 per cent toward the fog colour, so the reflection is always duller and cooler than the thing. AND IT STOPS SHORT OF THE BASE BY ONE PROP RADIUS, leaving a thin band of un-mirrored dark silt between object and reflection. That gap is the whole trick, and it is the same trick as alpine.ts's snow cap stopping shy of the eave: the dark strip is what says THIS IS SITTING ON SOMETHING. A reflection joined to its object is a smear; a reflection with a shadow gap is a mirror. THE COROLLARY, for the table, which is the one prop that is 95 per cent horizontal: every table section carries a white cloth that hangs 0.35 units PROUD of the dark oak board on both long sides, so from directly overhead the record reads as a continuous bright ribbon with a fine dark line down each edge, and it holds that continuity across the joints between separately-merged sections. A bare board from this camera is a plank, and a plank is the same value as the flats. If a section in this kit has a board and no overhanging cloth, the section is not finished. AND THE BOUND ON THE COST: only props on the FLATS get painted reflections. Island ground is grass and mud, so the island props get none, which keeps the reflection set to an authored list rather than to every scattered prop in the level.
- **Institution in denial.** THE ADJUDICATOR. He is here from the record-keeping body to verify a claim, he arrived on the first boat this morning, and he is not from here. Brand chips escalate the office rather than the man: tier 0 "📏 THE ADJUDICATION", tier 1 "⚖️ RULING", tier 2 "🏆 THE RECORD". THE FLAVOUR OF THE DENIAL, and why it is different in kind from all five: Maple's mayor DENIES; Pirate's management COVERS; GAME DAY's booth TRANSLATES catastrophe into play-by-play; Lantern's PA cannot perceive and simply THANKS; Powder's closures desk FILES it as administration and never breaks format. All five are insiders — they love the place and they lie about it, or they are its own machinery. THE ADJUDICATOR ARBITRATES. He does not deny the void, cover it, translate it, thank it or file it: he RULES ON IT, against a rulebook that does not mention it, and finds each time that the book is silent and the attempt therefore continues. His denial is IMPARTIALITY. To call the hole a disaster would be to take the town's side, and an adjudicator does not take sides — so the one thing he cannot say is the true thing, and that is the joke: a man refusing to be the only person in the estuary who is allowed to panic. He does not raise his voice because raising it would be advocacy. He is also, quietly, the warmest institution in the game: alone of the six he is trying to GIVE the town something, and at the end he gives it. EIGHT SAMPLE HEADLINES, metered against qa/newsstyle.mjs (tier 0 zero exclamation marks, tier 1 at most one, tier 2 "!!" or nothing but never a lone "!", 78 characters at worst-case token fill, tokens only {D}{M}{F}{P}{R}{S}, {S} tier 2 only, no article before {D}). The sign-off is the only line in the game where an institution GRANTS something: everybody is up on the rock, counted twice, and the record stands.

- **Risk, in the designer's own words.** FOUR HONEST ONES, in order of how likely they are to sink it. (1) IT IS THE SECOND WORLD WITH SAND, BOATS AND WATER IN THE THUMBNAIL. Pirate Bay is already the beach world, and a stranger scrolling past a picker card of pale ground, hulls and a shoreline may file this next to World 2 before reading anything. I have pushed as hard as I can the other way — a cold northern estuary instead of the tropics, dawn instead of high sun, rose-pewter silt instead of turquoise and gold, no palms, six islands instead of one headland, and a ground that mirrors rather than glares — but this is the risk I would take to art direction first, before a line of code, with the two picker cards side by side. If the answer is that they rhyme, the estuary should go fresh-water and inland (a reservoir drawn down, a lake bed) and lose the sea entirely. (2) THE DAWN RIG IS THE LOWEST-CONTRAST RIG IN THE GAME AND THIS REPO HAS BEEN BURNED BY EXACTLY THAT. GAME DAY's first pass measured mean frame luminance 0.280 against Maple's 0.626 and was ruled the wrong register for six-year-olds; Lantern shipped with 26 per cent of the frame crushed under 25/255 and had to be pulled back from exposure 1.42 to 1.24. A hemi-dominant dawn is not dark, but it IS flat: a weak key means weak form separation, and forms in this game are held apart by the warm-key-against-cool-fill counter-light that a weak key largely disarms. This world will lean on the fill and on the signal reds harder than any other, and it may still measure as mush. It should be metered with qa/shippedlook and the luminance histogram before a single prop is authored, on a grey-box island, and the rig should be tuned to a number rather than to a description. (3) THE PAINTED REFLECTION HARD-COUPLES THE GROUND BAKE TO PROP PLACEMENT. Lantern already pays this — its lantern pools are painted at each lantern's own position — so the machinery exists, but a pool is round and needs only a position, while a reflection needs a direction, a length and a colour, so it is strictly more work per prop and a prop that moves must move its reflection or the world develops ghosts. ### THE BIG LAP — from the "engine" angle

**one lap. mind the gap.**

RINGWOOD is a village built all the way round a hole. Nobody remembers why the hole is there and everybody is extremely proud of it: there are railings, there are benches facing out into it, there is a coin telescope pointed at nothing. Once a year, at first light, the whole village goes round the outside — the fun run, the fete, and the Ringwood & District Miniature Railway, which has right of way — and this is the year a small purple hole enters the field without a number on.

- **Silhouette.** A RING, seen from directly overhead: an irregular oval landmass about 7,400 world units across with a 3,700 x 3,100 hole punched clean through it, and open space visible through the hole. The hole is deliberately OFF-CENTRE — pushed about 640 units north-west of the island's centre — so the band of land is 4,390 units wide in the south-east where the fete field sits and pinches to 2,520 in the north-west at THE NARROWS. It refuses, by name: Maple Isle's fat blob on a block grid (this has no interior at all to put a grid in); Pirate Bay's hooked headland cradling water (the hole here is not water, it is nothing, and it never needs a bridge to be walkable because you go round it); GAME DAY's lopsided plateau (that shape is one sightline pointing at one building — this one has no straight sightline anywhere, and its longest view is across the gap at the far side of your own island); Lantern Night's waisted valley with a street up it (a street has two ends, and this world has none); Powder Pass's concentric bowl round an off-centre lake (the closest relative and the one to be most careful of — a bowl is a filled disc composed inward toward a centre, and this is a disc with the centre REMOVED and every composition running tangentially, never radially). And it is not a perfect annulus: the outer coast swings from 4,900 to 5,500 units of radius and carries a squared-off shoulder in the south-east for the fete field, so the outline never closes into a symbol.
- **Why it is a new shape.** In the first second, before a child reads a single prop, there is a hole in the middle of the island with stars in it. Every other world in this game is a solid piece of ground with a coastline round the outside; this is the only one with a coastline on the INSIDE, and the fixed 225-degree camera puts both of them in the same frame — you can see the far rim of your own island rising in the top of the shot, tiny, upside down, across a gap of open space. No repaint can produce that. It also reads instantly as a rule the child already understands from every toy they own: things go round. The engine gets there almost free — island.ts builds the ground as a `THREE.Shape` + `ShapeGeometry` (island.ts:3070), and a Shape carries holes natively, so the hole is `shape.holes.push(new THREE.Path(...))`; the custom UV loop below it is derived from vertex positions, not from a ring walk, so it needs no change; the underside cap is `topGeo.clone()` and inherits the hole; and the bake needs nothing at all, because island.ts states three separate times (lines 1139, 1280, 1423) that texels outside the coastline are never sampled since the ground mesh IS the silhouette. The land predicate is the shape `onBayLand` already ships: `pointInPoly(outer) && !pointInPoly(inner)`.
- **Landmark.** THE VIADUCT. A stone-and-iron bridge carrying the ring road and the miniature railway together straight across the hole, pier to pier, with nothing under it but stars. It is the biggest single prop in the game, it is the one thing that makes the silhouette a Θ instead of an O, and from directly overhead it is a ladder of girders with open space showing between every rung — the only prop in this game a player can see THROUGH. It is the last thing eaten, at the end of the last lap, with the band standing on it, and when it goes the island is a plain ring again: the finale is the only moment in the game where eating something changes what the island's outline looks like from above. Structurally it is a spur of the land slab (a 1,500-unit-wide causeway, 75 units in 3D — four times a WORLD ENDER's width, so it is a broad avenue and never a tightrope) carrying an authored prop; the slab's Shape simply gets two D-shaped holes instead of one, which `ShapeGeometry` handles the same way it handles one.
- **Palette and light.** CHALK WHITE, DEW GREEN, ROSE GOLD, and HI-VIS YELLOW as the one hard accent — with bunting red and the little train's pillarbox red doing the small work. The hour is DAWN, and the slot is genuinely free: WORLD_LIGHT (prototype3d.ts:734) holds maple and pirate at flat daylight, gameday at a 40-degree amber rake, lantern at a moonless night lit by its own props, powder at blue winter dusk. Nothing in HOURS (:907) is a low, pale, high-key first light — maple's "bright morning", pirate's "morning tide" and powder's "cold bright morning" are all brightness variations on their own daylight key, not a different sun. The danger is reading as GAME DAY's golden hour, and the defence is measurable rather than stylistic: golden hour is a SATURATED, high-contrast rig (amber key 0xffd9a8 at 2.55, mean luminance 0.331, long black shadows raking across tarmac); dawn here inverts every one of those terms — a low key at modest strength in pale rose, an unusually bright and very pale hemisphere carrying a white-gold horizon, shadows that are long but soft and lilac because the sky is doing most of the lighting, and a mean luminance around 0.70, which would make this the brightest world in the game by a distance. Five worlds are mid-to-dark; the sixth being pearl-pale is worth a lot on a store page full of thumbnails. The one saturated thing in frame is the bunting, and the hi-vis marshals — a colour no other world owns — spaced at fixed pitch the whole way round, so from overhead the island wears a dotted yellow line.
- **The overhead rule.** THE CHALK RULE. This is the only world where large parts of the frame are pure black — the hole — and the camera looks straight down at it, so everything standing near the inner rim is silhouetted against nothing instead of against ground. alpine.ts's snow cap exists because a white roof on white ground vanishes; the same fact bites the other way here, because a DARK prop on the brink is a hole inside a hole and the eye loses it completely. So: nothing within 600 world units of the inner rim may be painted from the dark half of the palette. The railing is chalk white, the benches are whitewashed, the telescope is cream, the signs are white on white, the marshals on that side wear the hi-vis, and the ground bake runs a hard chalk line along the very lip all the way round — the way a village actually paints the edge of a drop. The rule is enforced as a distance test, not as taste: a prop factory in the kit that puts a dark colour on a brink-class prop is not finished. And the same line does double duty as the child's safety read — the pale ring says EDGE from any zoom, at any speed, without a word of UI. (The corollary, for the same reason: the viaduct is drawn in OPENWORK. A solid deck over the hole photographs as a grey bar filling the empty middle; girders with black between them read as a bridge.)
- **Institution in denial.** THE LAP BOARD — the Ringwood & District timing hut. Two volunteers, a clock, and a board of hooks with numbers on. It is not a mayor, a tannoy, a commentary booth, a recording or a closures desk: it is a RESULTS SERVICE, and it can only perceive the world as classification. The closures desk files cancellations; this one files achievements, which is warmer and considerably funnier, because everything that happens gets read back as somebody doing well. It has three states and they are all the same voice: PROVISIONAL, PROVISIONAL (AMENDED), and FINAL. Tier 0 doubt: an unnumbered entrant has joined the field and would they please see the desk. Tier 1 alarm: the course has been shortened, by agreement, and the desk would like to stress that this was agreed. Tier 2: every category has now been won. Its finest move is that the void's actual behaviour — going round the island at speed, taking everything with it — is, from a lap board's point of view, a very good time. And the sign-off, which is the whole institution in one line: everybody is accounted for. The marshals counted them at the gate and counted them again at the end and got the same number both times, which the desk considers the important thing.
- **Districts.** THE START LINE — the widest ground on the island, south-east: the finish gantry over the road, the timing hut, a funnel of barriers and a lot of people who have already stopped.; THE FETE FIELD — dewy mown grass, white marquee, tombola, cake table, coconut shy, deck chairs in rows facing the road, and the one district where the crowd is standing still on purpose.; RINGWOOD — the village proper on the outer rim: eight stone cottages, the hall, the post office and the Ring o' Bells, all with their backs to the coast and their front doors to the lap, because nobody here builds facing away from the race.; THE BRINK — the band of ground along the hole itself, all the way round: chalk-white railing, benches facing OUT into space, a coin telescope, and eleven signs asking you to mind the gap. The only district in the game with nothing on one side of it.; THE NARROWS — the north-west pinch, 2,520 units of land between the two coasts, where the road and the railway run side by side between straw-bale walls and two marshals wave the same flag at each other across the gap.; THE PADDOCK — the miniature railway's yard on the west: engine shed, coaling stage, water tower, a turntable, and a stack of sleepers that a child could and will climb on.; THE ALLOTMENTS — the south-west quiet lane: greenhouses, bean rows, water butts, nine sheds and one prize marrow under a cloth. The visual rest between the yard and the fete, and the only place on the lap that is not shouting.; THE OUTER SHOULDER — the catch-all rim between the road and the outer cliff: hedgerow, gorse, parked cars in the long grass, and the hedge-height view of everything else.
- **Beats.** 🏃 | They're off! | nobody agreed on how many laps · 🚂 | Train coming through! | it has right of way. it always has · 🎈 | Balloon release! | they will come down somewhere · 🏁 | LAST LAP! | everybody is coming back round
- **Errands.** Everybody is doing a lap, and that single sentence is the reason this world is the best geometry the errand system has ever been given. `addWanderer`'s opt-in `leg` (life.ts:2597) walks a straight leg, dwells 2.5-6 seconds, and picks the next with 0.9 radians of heading persistence — and its documented failure is a leg that meets a boundary, which on Pirate Bay produced home-and-back ping-pong, 72 degrees per second of turning and a drift of 0.17. On a ring, the tangent NEVER points at a coast. Give every walker a tangential opening heading and a leg of ~90 3D units against a generous tether, and the three-point leg validity walk passes essentially always, the legs chain into a long shallow arc (net/path stays high because 90 units of a ~750-unit circumference is nearly a straight line), and each leg is a completed journey by purpose.mjs's own definition: leave by 15, settle inside 3, hold 1.5. I would expect drift well north of 0.80 against the 0.30 bar and a traveller share far past a third, and the fix if it comes in short is one line — bias `retarget`'s `a0` toward the tangent instead of toward home — not a system. The destinations are real and named, so a child can see the errand as well as measure it: runners going round (the whole field, all one way, which is the level's signature image from above); marshals walking out to their numbered posts at the start and being relieved at the end; the tea urn being carried the WRONG way round the lap by two people who will not be told; kids chasing the little train from the shed to the viaduct and back; the cake table restocked from the marquee every ninety seconds; the balloon crew walking a full lap to the launch field with the basket on a trolley; a dog doing two laps unaccompanied. ### DAWN PATROL — from the "child" angle

**get them before they go up**

"SKYLARK FIELD is a disused grass-and-concrete airfield floating in space, and once a year, for one morning, a hundred hot air balloons come to it. A match is that morning: from the trailers arriving in the dark to the mass ascension at sunrise, compressed to three minutes. The child is turned loose as a purple hole in a field of the biggest, softest, most colourful objects anybody has ever built — things four adults can barely carry, that lie on the grass like spilled paint, stand up to the height of a church, and are all, at the end, trying very hard to leave. The joke is that this is a world where the food escapes UPWARDS. Eat the balloon first and you get the balloon; eat the ropes first and it goes up without you, and everybody waves at it. That one rule is the whole level, a six-year-old works it out in ten seconds, and it is the thing they explain to another child at school."

- **Silhouette.** "A BLUNT THREE-POINTED DELTA WITH CONCAVE BAYS — and what it refuses to repeat is every convex outline in the game. Maple Isle is a fat blob, Pirate Bay is a hooked headland with one concavity, Game Day is a lopsided plateau, Lantern Night is a waisted valley, Powder Pass is a wide oval bowl. Four of those five bulge outward everywhere; the fifth bulges outward everywhere except its one hook. SKYLARK FIELD bulges outward in exactly three places and is bitten INWARD in three: space cuts into this island three times, which no other silhouette in the game allows. From directly overhead it is a squat three-armed shape, roughly 7,000 across and 8,200 tall, with blunt promontories at about (6150,1500) north, (9400,9600) south-east and (2700,9300) south-west, and the coast between them scalloping in to (8100,4200), (6000,9800) and (3900,4400). Each promontory is a runway threshold and each tip stays 2,600-3,000 wide so a full-grown WORLD ENDER can turn round out there; the narrowest thing a void must cross anywhere on the island is about 3,200, comfortably over the ~700 a WORLD ENDER needs and near the 3,900 Powder set as its comfort floor. Drawn ON that shape, and half the reason it reads instantly, are THREE STRAIGHT CONCRETE BARS crossing in the middle-north — runway 03/21 from (3300,9000) to (6300,2000), 09/27 from (3100,5600) to (9000,7000), and 15/33 from (5100,2600) to (8900,9200) — each 1,000 world units wide with painted centreline dashes, plus a CLOSED PERIMETER TRACK looping the entire coast about 700 in from the rim at half-width 200. So the island's outline is drawn on the island. Nothing else in this game has a straight edge at that scale (Maple's grid is thin streets between blocks, not 1,000-wide slabs), and nothing else has a road that circumnavigates the world. Two engine notes so this is not hand-waving: the runway half-width of 500 makes it the only surface in the game wider than a maxed-out player (R_CAP 18 in 3D = 360 world units of radius against a 1,000-wide strip), which is deliberate and is the opposite call from bay.ts's boardwalk and gameday's concourse, both cut to half-width 170 precisely because they were wider than a WORLD ENDER — a runway is supposed to dwarf you. And the perimeter track being CLOSED means its polyline must repeat its first point as its last, or distToPath leaves the same kind of hole in the ring that gameday.ts records finding 609 units wide due east of its bowl."
- **Why it is a new shape.** "Five things land in the first second, and none of them is a colour. (1) THE OUTLINE IS CONCAVE IN THREE PLACES. Every other island in this game is a bulge; this one has space cutting into it three times, so the first read is 'that is not a lump, that has arms'. (2) THERE ARE STRAIGHT LINES ACROSS THE MIDDLE. Three pale 1,000-unit concrete bars crossing at hard angles, with white dashes painted down them. No other world has a straight edge of any size — Maple's grid is thin streets, everything else is curves — so a triangle of rulers laid over the land is unmistakable at a glance and unmistakably man-made. (3) THE ISLAND'S EDGE IS DRAWN ON THE ISLAND. The perimeter track traces the whole coast 700 units in, so the silhouette survives even where the rim falls into dawn shadow; the outline reads as a thin grey three-pointed ring whether or not the coast does. (4) THE COLOUR IS INVERTED. Every other world carries its identity in the GROUND — autumn ground, sand, tarmac and turf, night ground, snow. This one's ground is deliberately drained (cold wet green-grey, grey-lilac concrete) and all the saturation in the frame sits in ninety objects lying on top of it. A cold field with bright confetti on it is not any of the five, at thumbnail size, in a picker shelf. (5) THE SHADOWS RAKE THE OTHER WAY. All five shipped rigs key from negative X (WORLD_LIGHT, prototype3d.ts:734); this one keys from the east. Every shadow in the frame points the opposite direction to every shadow the player has seen in this game. And one that lands in the first ten seconds rather than the first one: THE SHAPE CHANGES DURING THE MATCH. The balloons are authored in four stages and the ratio walks forward, so a field of long flat crescents on the grass slowly becomes a field of standing domes. No other world's composition moves. A stranger scrolling past a still of this sees a shape they have not seen; a stranger watching six seconds of it sees a world stand up."
- **Landmark.** "THE WHALE — registration G-WAIL, a forty-metre special-shape balloon of a blue whale, and the biggest single object in the game. For the first half of the match she is LYING DOWN: a colossal cobalt crescent of fabric spilled across the launch circle at the runway crossing, 1,400 world units nose to tail, with her tail flukes flat on the concrete and four crew walking her seams. From directly overhead, at dawn, on grey concrete, she is the most striking image this game has ever produced — and the child can walk the whole length of her before they are big enough to eat any of it. At beat 3 she cold-inflates and stands: a fifteen-metre blue dome with a white belly and a small, absurd, entirely convincing eye. At the finale her tether pins are pulled and she starts to go. She is the last thing eaten, and the child who gets her gets her in the two seconds after her basket leaves the ground. Her precinct is the painted launch circle (2,200 across) and owns both her footprints — lying and standing — plus her ground crew, her fan trailer, her pins and the commentary trestle, so eating her takes the circle out from under her rather than leaving a ring of surviving rope. What the child says: 'there was a WHALE and I ate it just as it was leaving.'"
- **Palette and light.** "FIRST LIGHT, about 5:40 on a Saturday in early June — the half hour before sunrise, which is when balloon meets actually happen, because it is the only time the air is calm. FOUR COLOURS. (1) PERIWINKLE — the west half of the sky and every shadow on the field. Dawn shadows are long and they are sky-coloured, the same physics alpine.ts's BLUE SHADOW RULE is built on, so nothing here uses a neutral grey either. (2) APRICOT — a low, narrow band on the east horizon and a warm rake across the tops of things: the tower's glazing, the crown of every standing balloon, the wet concrete. It is a thin colour and it must stay thin; the moment apricot spreads past the top third this becomes Game Day's golden hour. (3) WET GREEN-GREY — the grass, dewed and cold, desaturated to about half a summer lawn, with a pale grey-lilac for the concrete and chalk-white for the painted markings. (4) THE BALLOONS, WHICH ARE EVERY SATURATED COLOUR THERE IS. - **The overhead rule.** "THE CROWN RULE — A BALLOON IS MOSTLY ITS CROWN, AND A FIELD OF CIRCLES IS NOT A COMPOSITION. From directly overhead a standing balloon is a disc. A disc of one colour is a dot; ninety dots is confetti, and confetti photographs as a texture, not as objects — which is this world's version of the black rectangle nightmarket.ts learned the hard way and the white-roof-on-white-ground problem alpine.ts's eave line solves. Four enforcements, and a factory that skips them is not finished. (1) GORES REACH THE CROWN. Every envelope is built as vertical coloured panels radiating from the top, and the seams must run all the way to the crown ring, so from straight above the disc reads as a PINWHEEL with radial spokes. Spokes are what give a circle size, rotation and form when you cannot see its side. (2) THE CROWN RING IS A DARK HOLE. The very top is an open dark circle — the disc's pupil. It is the one thing that says 'this is a hollow bag standing up' rather than a painted coin, and it is the exact structural equivalent of alpine's dark slate strip at the eave. (3) THE SKIRT IS THE EAVE LINE. A standing envelope hides its own basket, burner and crew under itself. So the mouth is lifted clear of the basket on the burner frame and the skirt is a contrasting dark ring, giving the overhead read disc → dark ring → basket → four tiny people. Without that ring a balloon has no people and this world has no crowd. (4) THE HARDER HALF: THE FIELD MUST NOT BE ALL UPRIGHT BALLOONS. A twenty-metre envelope at a 225° down-angle is an opaque object that eats the frame and hides the child's own void. So every balloon is authored in one of FOUR STAGES — BAGGED (a fridge-sized roll), SPILLED (a long flat gore-striped crescent on the grass), COLD (a fat lying sausage with a fan at its mouth) and STANDING — held at roughly 5:4:3:2. Stages one to three are LOW: they read as enormous coloured shapes lying ON the ground, which is what an overhead camera actually wants, and the standing ones are punctuation. Then the ratio walks forward across the three minutes, so THE WORLD STANDS UP AS THE CHILD GROWS. No other world changes silhouette during a match; this one is authored to."
- **Institution in denial.** "THE MET BALLOON — the meet's own field radio, read from the tower balcony by the BALLOONMEISTER, Mr. Pym, whose single job is deciding whether it is safe to fly. Not a politician (Maple), not hospitality (Pirate), not two commentators calling a game (Game Day), not a recording that cannot perceive (Lantern), not a closures desk reading a list (Powder). Pym's denial is TECHNICAL: he has instruments, they are excellent, and they are answering the wrong question with total confidence. He processes the void as CONDITIONS — a meteorological feature and a surface obstruction — and files it under the only headings the briefing form has. Tier 0, DOUBT: he notes a large violet feature moving across the field at four knots, observes that it is not forecast, and declares conditions good. Tier 1, ALARM: he believes it now, and it changes nothing, because his criteria have not changed — the theory stops and the advice starts in the same measured voice: crews are asked to keep it on their left, and the launch window is extended. Tier 2, PANIC: broadcasting from the tower balcony, which is the last standing thing, he declares the finest flying conditions in the history of the meet — and by his own criteria he is RIGHT, because every obstruction on the field has been removed and visibility is unlimited in all directions. That is the engine of the joke and it is unique to this world: as the child eats, the field gets flatter, emptier and clearer, so the institution's own instruments keep telling it things are IMPROVING. He never breaks format; he reads the briefing to the end every time, including the wind, the cloud base, the NOTAMs, and 'have a good flight'. The sign-off writes the everybody-is-fine rule for free: 'All crews accounted for. Ninety-one balloons airborne. That is a record and the committee wishes to note it.'"

- **Risk, in the designer's own words.** "Six honest ones, worst first. (1) THE STANDING BALLOONS OCCLUDE, AND THAT COULD SINK THE WORLD. A twenty-metre envelope viewed at 225° down covers a lot of ground and can hide the child's own void — the single thing this game may never do. The four-stage authoring in the crown rule is a mitigation, not a proof, and it needs a real screenshot pass at 430×932 before anyone writes a line of the land module. If the answer is 'balloons must be shorter than they are', the world survives; if it is 'the field can only hold four standing at once', the world's whole promise thins out. (2) A FIELD OF DOTS READS AS A TEXTURE. Ninety discs on flat grass is exactly the dense-flat-district trap the gameday lighting note describes, where a low sun and density fight each other and the whole precinct goes to mush. The pinwheel gores and the three-colour limit are the defence and they have to survive at phone resolution, which is a measurement, not an argument. (3) THE MASS ASCENSION IS A MECHANIC, NOT A BEAT, AND IT TAKES FOOD AWAY. Nothing else in this game removes edibles from the child. It could easily read as the game cheating — the exact thing a six-year-old resents. It needs a long telegraph (a balloon inflates fully, burns twice, crew step back, THEN it goes), balloons leaving one at a time and never in a wave, and probably a hard rule that the last handful never leave. It also has to remove departing props from the edible set cleanly or the score and par arithmetic drift under it. (4) PAR IS UNMEASURED AND THIS WORLD IS NATURALLY SPARSE. Powder starved its child driver at 403-3,253 points on the sparse bake, and qa/_edcount.mjs found why: 843 edibles against Maple's 5,790. A grass airfield is mostly grass. The balloon count has to be genuinely high (90+ envelopes, each a real edible, plus their baskets, fans, cylinders and pegs as separate meals) and the hangars, breakfast row and arrivals have to be dressed hard, or this is a starvation level. My guess is par lands between Maple and Powder, around 55-70k, but that is a guess and WORLD_PAR takes measured numbers only. (5) DAWN IS A DIM RIG AND DIM IS THE THING THE OWNER ALREADY REJECTED. Lantern was retuned because 26% of the frame sat under 25/255. A dawn rig has to land nearer Powder's 3-6%. The reason I think it can is that lantern's murk was measured as albedo-bound — a night ground authored near-black — and this ground is pale concrete and dewed grass, which is the opposite problem.
