## 1. The void turns whitish when he is about to be eaten

> "When the void is about to get eaten he turns like whitish for color. Why?"

**ANSWERED, and it is the EYES, not the body.** No body uniform is driven by
mood at all — the purple never changes. What changes is `MOODS.scared`
(`void3d.ts`): `wide: 1.16` enlarges the sclera and `pupil: 0.55` shrinks the
pupil to just over half, so the eye flips from mostly-DARK to mostly-WHITE. At
play size, two big white discs on a small purple ball read as the whole
character going pale. A blue sweat drop and `blush: 0.3` (less pink) finish it.

Rendered side by side: `qa/out/mood/now/mood-cruise.png` against
`mood-scared.png`. Cruise is dark-eyed and warm; scared is two white saucers.

`scared` fires when any rival more than 1.15x your radius is within
`R + rv.r + 16` (`prototype3d.ts`), i.e. it is the game's "something can eat
you" tell.

**So it is authored, and it is not landing.** The owner did not read white
saucer eyes as fear — he read a colour change and asked why. The classic
cartoon version of this keeps the pupil BLACK and merely small; here it also
goes grey, which drops the contrast that would have said "eyes". That is the
thing to fix, not the whiteness itself.

**THE OWNER HAS ANSWERED, and the answer was neither of my two options.** Put
to him as an art call, he kept the face and moved the question:

> "Re the white - I like when the void gets nervous. That face is solid. But
> could we improve it - right now it just panicky on one void family when it's
> larger. What if it was any void that's larger. It seems only 1 void is ever
> hostile. I don't want to create this shit show of every void attacking you.
> But there should be some sense of like I need to be on my toes somewhat.
> Again a kids game right. The other voids are just there."

So the saucer eyes stay. **The fear face is APPROVED** and goes on the hands-off
list; what was wrong was never the face, it was that a child almost never sees
it fire for anyone except the hunter.

And the reason is arithmetic, not intent. The scared mood already fires for ANY
rival above 1.15x — but only inside `R + rv.r + 16`, which at play sizes is a
few of your own body-lengths. The hunter is the only void in the game that
deliberately closes that distance; the other four graze props on their own
errands and reach it by coincidence. The tell was authored for everyone and
delivered by one character.

The answer shipped on 2026-08-25 is THE LOOK (`rivals.ts`): a rival bigger than
you that finds you within 62 units stops what it is doing, holds a beat, says
one line in its own voice and takes one ring in its own colour. No pursuit, no
charge — walking into one still costs the ordinary bite it always did. One look
per rival every 9-16 seconds and never two at once, because the owner ruled out
the shit show in the same breath as he asked for the tension.

`qa/rivalnotice.mjs` holds it to a band at both ends and, since it also samples
the gate, it measures the very thing this section diagnoses: how much of a match
a bigger void is even close enough to react to you.

## 2. Who can eat him, and what does it cost?

> "Also can other voids eat him or just that one? Is there a penalty for being
> eaten?"

**CORRECTED, 2026-08-25. The answer is: in practice, JUST THAT ONE.**

What I wrote first — and it was wrong in the way that matters — was: "it is
not one specific void, it is anyone more than 1.2x your radius". That is a
true description of the GATE. `rivals.ts` does fire the bite on
`rv.r > pr * 1.2 && dp < rv.r * 1.05 && rv.biteCd <= 0`, with no reference to
which rival it is.

It is a false description of the GAME, because nothing except the hunter can
ever satisfy it. The size ceiling a few hundred lines up is
`softCap = max(min(START_R + 0.02t, 1.6), pr * 0.80)`, `hardCap` is `softCap`
for every non-hunter — the two escapes that lift it, `want * 1.04` and
`stuffCap`, are both inside `if (isHunter)` — and it is clamped every single
frame. Clearing 1.2x the player therefore requires `1.6 > 1.2 * pr`, i.e. a
player still under radius 1.333. The family joins at 0.62x you and grows
slowly; you pass 1.333 inside the first ten seconds.

So the family is capped BELOW you, always, by design. Measured independently:
`qa/rivalnotice.mjs` sampled the same condition twice a second across Maple
and Pirate and found it open **0% of the time** in both.

This is also the whole of why "only 1 void is ever hostile" — see item 1.

There are two grades, and the difference is large:

| | ordinary rival | the HUNTER, mid-hunt |
|---|---|---|
| radius | x0.90 | x0.85, floored one full rung down the form ladder |
| score | nothing | up to 8% stolen, capped at 1200 |
| form | unchanged | **demoted** — the HUD name changes |
| cooldown | 9s | 12s, then she breaks off and wallows |

The stolen score is BANKED by her, so it is winnable back by eating her later —
which is the only event in the game that lifts the growth law.

You also cannot be chain-bitten: `biteMercy` gives 2.5s after an ordinary bite
and 3.2s after the hunter's.

The "ordinary rival" column is therefore **unreachable in normal play**. It is
what would happen if a family member ever got big enough, and one cannot.

**Nothing to fix here** — but note the demotion was silently rewarding the
player until 2026-08-24, when the EVOLVED ceremony was found firing on the way
back up. That is fixed.

## 6. Space does not look like space

> "The background of the space /galaxy - it's like this weird faded color. It
> should pop. It doesn't give me the illusion this is in space. Would be cool to
> see some planets or something. Something different for each level maybe."

**ANSWERED, AND IT WAS NOT THE SKY.** "This weird faded color" has a value:
**#5c4987**, measured off a real shipped frame (`qa/out/person/maple_side_2.png`,
a personsheet shot that missed its subject and photographed nothing but
background). Saturation 0.460, luminance range **0.008**, and **zero** pixels
below L 0.06. A range of eight thousandths across a whole frame is not a sky
with a colour problem. It is a flat fill.

The painted sky asset is not at fault — on its own it measures saturation
0.754, range 0.463, a quarter of it true black. What was flattening it is an
**additive violet plane 1207 units across, lying at y = -3**, depth-tested so
it is hidden under the island and visible only past the coastline — that is,
exactly and solely where space is. Tested causally by hiding and restoring it
in the live scene mid-measurement:

| | saturation | range | true black |
|---|---|---|---|
| as shipped | 0.485 | 0.294 | 1% |
| halo hidden | 0.862 | 0.063 | **95%** |

Two faults, both fixed. Its texture was built with `new THREE.CanvasTexture()`,
which defaults to `NoColorSpace`, so its sRGB bytes reached the shader as
linear — 1.7x the red and 2.4x the green, added to a sky whose own value is
about 0.01. And its reach: full alpha ran to ~283 units against a coast at
~272. It is now sRGB-decoded and 1.35x the island instead of 2.1x, so the
falloff finishes just past the coast. Tightened, not deleted — it is what
stops the island reading as a cutout.

**The starfield was outside the frustum entirely.** 1500 stars, with a
magnitude curve, four colour temperatures and a two-sine twinkle, all
carefully authored and never once seen. `ph` ran to `Math.PI * 0.6`, which
stops 18 degrees ABOVE level, and a y-squash and a -40 offset flattened what
remained into a disc hanging over the island. This camera only looks DOWN — 46
degrees at spawn, 65 by VOID TITAN, on a 32-degree lens — so nothing above 27
degrees BELOW horizontal is ever on screen and the horizon never is. Fixed.

**Planets, per world, because he asked.** Amber banded giant and a cold moon
over Maple; a ringed teal world over Pirate; a magenta dusk giant over Game
Day; a red lantern-moon over Lantern; a ringed ice world over Powder. Sprites
with the terminator, bands and rings painted in — at 600-900 units a lit
sphere and a painted disc are indistinguishable, and the scene's sun is aimed
at the island, so a real sphere out there would be lit from the wrong side.
Placed between -34 and -62 degrees elevation, inside the only band the camera
can see.

### THE NUMBERS, AFTER — measured 2026-08-25, Maple at the coast

| | the shipped frame, before | now |
|---|---|---|
| saturation | 0.460 | **0.708** |
| luminance range (P95-P5) | 0.008 | **0.698** |
| true black (L < 0.06) | 0.0% | **25%** |
| planets on screen | none | 2, covering 3.6% of the frame |

The range is the one that matters and the one an intensity slider cannot move:
eight thousandths is a flat fill however bright you make it. It is now 0.698.

`qa/skypop.mjs` still reports the halo A/B beside those, because the plane is
worth watching: hidden, the sky reads sat 0.673 / range 0.614 / **44%** black
against 25% with it. The remaining gap is the rim glow doing its job at the
island's edge, which is what stops the island reading as a cutout.

### WHAT THE PHOTOGRAPHS CAUGHT THAT THE NUMBERS DID NOT

The numbers said the sky was fixed. `qa/out/space/` said otherwise, three
times, and one of the three was me.

**The planets were the sky.** Sized against the 32-degree VERTICAL field, which
made 18-20 degree discs. A phone held upright has a HORIZONTAL field of about
15 degrees, so Maple's giant measured **58.3% of the frame** — wall to wall.
Giants are 7-8 degrees now, moons 2-3.

**I misread the first photograph and the instrument corrected me.** I called the
planet washed out to blue-violet and began cutting the halo's alpha to fix it.
Forcing the sprite in front of everything and sampling the same pixels returned
rgb(144,102,59) against rgb(140,105,72) as-rendered. Identical: nothing was in
front of it, and it had been warm amber all along. What I was looking at was
its own dark limb filling the frame. My eye invented a defect; the A/B killed
it before it became a "fix".

**The hue rotation never did what its comments said.** `hue: 145 // swung to
sea-teal` photographed dark BROWN. `hue: 95 // aurora greens` photographed dark
MAGENTA. CSS `hue-rotate` is the SVG luminance-preserving matrix, not an HSL
rotation, and on a base this saturated it drives a channel negative which the
canvas clamps to zero — 83% of Pirate's pixels, 78% of Game Day's. No angle
avoids it. It is a canvas `color` composite now: hue and saturation from the
fill, luminosity from the painting. Powder tints at 0.62 so its own greens
survive as aurora instead of flattening into one blue.

That last one is the whole of "something different for each level maybe". Until
today three of the five worlds were variations on brown.

### AND THE MEASUREMENT THAT OUTRANKS ALL OF IT — AN OWNER CALL

**At real spawn size, sky is 0.0% of the frame. At the spawn, and standing
directly on the coast.** The camera is pitched 46 degrees down and the frame
reaches about fourteen units past the hero, so a small void simply cannot see
past its own patch of ground. Space only arrives as you grow: at radius 10 by
a coastline it is 44% of the frame.

That may be exactly right — space as the reward for growing, and the reason
the late game feels different. But it means none of the work above reaches a
child in the first minute, and the fix is not an art fix. It is one of:
raising the camera, shrinking the island, or accepting that space is a
late-game reveal and selling it somewhere a child does look — the menu, the
picker, the results card. **That is his decision, not mine.**

## What "get a team on quality" means here

The studio's eight teams review SURFACES. Nothing in it owns the things above,
which are mostly BEHAVIOUR — a collision response, an effect that fires too
often, a word in a string, a family that never reacts to you. Nothing in the
gate could see any of the six either, which is why the owner found them and the
gate did not.

**So the ninth team is not a review meeting. It is `--profile=quality` in
`qa/gate.mjs`.** A team that meets and gives opinions cannot fail a build; an
instrument can. Each step has a band at BOTH ends, because every one of these
fails in two directions and the owner named both directions himself — too
little and nothing changed, too much and it is "this shit show" for a
six-year-old.

Steps join `push` and `live` only once they have a green reading. A gate step
that has never passed is not protection, it is a blocked pipeline. On probation
as of 2026-08-25:
